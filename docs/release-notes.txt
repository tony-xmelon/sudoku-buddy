A fix for a page the app refused, and five things about how it asks you.

A CROSSED-OUT CANDIDATE IS NO LONGER READ AS A PRINTED CLUE

Photograph a booklet page part way through and the app would sometimes say the
printed digits do not make a solvable puzzle - on a page whose printed digits it
had in fact read perfectly. It was adding to them.

The squares at fault hold a pencilled candidate list that ran onto a second line,
with one of the figures crossed out. The cancel stroke runs past the figure at
both ends, so the ink it joins up with is the height of a printed digit while
sitting lower in the square than a printed digit ever sits. Every rule about where
a mark sits was guarding the top of the square, because that is where a list
starts, and none of them was guarding the bottom. Two of those in the bottom row
put two clues into the puzzle that nobody had printed.

A printed digit may now not sit that low unless it carries the weight of ink a
press leaves. Real clues do sit low - a page shot at an angle, a face with a long
tail - but they are printed, and print is heavier than pencil by a wide margin.

THE READING LAYER OPENS ITSELF WHEN THERE IS SOMETHING TO SETTLE

The squares being asked about are marked on the photograph, and the reading layer
is what shows those marks - so the app was asking a question and making you press
a button before you could see what it was about. If a scan comes back with
anything in doubt, that layer is already on.

SETTLING A SQUARE NOW STAYS SETTLED

Squares you had dealt with came back the moment you tapped anywhere else on the
photograph. The tap was handing back a copy of the puzzle from before you settled
them. It now reads what is actually on screen. The same fault was found and fixed
in the tutor's sideways swipe, where it would have undone a correction made while
the tutor was open.

THE MESSAGE KEEPS UP WITH WHAT YOU HAVE FIXED

"The printed digits do not make a solvable puzzle" was written once and then left
there while you fixed exactly that. Now, when the puzzle starts solving, it says
so - and the squares the app had named as likely culprits stop being marked,
because they were only ever suspects for as long as the puzzle was broken. A
complaint about the photograph itself, such as being too far away, still stands,
because correcting squares cannot make a picture sharper.

A BAR ONLY WHERE THERE IS DOUBT, AND IT MEANS ONE THING

Squares were wearing an amber bar and then telling you, when tapped, that they had
been read with complete confidence. The bar was saying two things at once: how
well a square was read, and whether the app wanted you to look at it. It now says
only the first, in green, amber or red, and the thickness says the second. Squares
read with confidence and not in question get no bar at all, so the few worth
finding are findable.

And when the app is genuinely unsure - a digit at a third sure is the best of nine
bad options rather than a reading - it no longer states what the square holds and
then undermines it with the number. It says it could not read the square, offers
its closest guess, and asks you to set it.
