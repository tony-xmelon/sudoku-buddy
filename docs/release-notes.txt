Four things this build does that the last one would not.

A PUZZLE PRINTED IN OLD-STYLE FIGURES NOW READS

The app finds the printed digits first, and it decides what is printed largely
by height: the print is the one thing on the page set at a single size, so
anything much shorter or taller is something else. That is true of most fonts
and not of all of them. In an old-style font - text figures, which newspapers
do set - the 1 and the 2 sit low, about three quarters the height of the 6 and
the 8, and every one of them was being filed as your handwriting. A puzzle
would come back missing a third of its clues, with nothing misread: the digits
were all recognised correctly and simply put in the wrong pile.

A whole rank of figures is now taken back, but only on evidence that it is a
rank rather than a hand. A font repeats itself exactly, so the short figures
all measure the same to within a hundredth, where handwriting on the same page
varies ten times as much. They also have to be as dark as the rest of the
print, be shorter than the print rather than taller, and leave the page with a
believable number of clues - so on a puzzle you have already filled in, where
the answers outnumber the clues, the rule cannot fire at all.

THE APP NO LONGER INVENTS DIGITS OUT OF NOTHING

A photograph of a screen came back with fourteen extra 1s in squares that were
empty. They were not digits: each was a smear about three grey levels darker
than the paper around it, where the real digits on that page stood a hundred
and thirty levels clear. Nothing in the check for a printed digit had ever
asked whether there was any ink there - every test was about how big the mark
was and where it sat, and a stripe of noise is the height of a digit and sits
where a digit sits.

Ink now has to be ink: a mark carrying less than a tenth of the depth of that
page's own print is not a mark anybody made. It is measured against the page
rather than as a fixed shade, because the pages that need it most are the faint
ones - a photograph of a screen can be four times fainter than newsprint all
over, and a fixed threshold would either pass the noise or throw away the
digits.

A PHOTOGRAPH OF A SCREEN READS BETTER

Photograph a monitor and the camera's sensor beats against the screen's pixels,
combing every square with fine vertical stripes. On their own they are
harmless. Where one touches a digit the two become a single mark with a
one-pixel thread standing out of the top of it - and the mark is then half
again as tall as the print, which is exactly what handwriting looks like.
Twelve of the thirty printed digits on one such photograph went that way.

On a page that is actually combed, a digit's height is now measured across the
part of it that is wider than a thread. The page has to be measurably striped
first: the two affected photographs are five times more striped than any other
page there is, so a photograph of paper takes precisely the path it took
before. Twelve wrong became one, and on a second such page five became one.

THE DRAWER NO LONGER OPENS ITSELF WHEN YOU TURN THE PHONE

Shut the list of puzzles, turn the phone from upright to sideways, and it
opened again by itself over whatever you were looking at. It has done that for
as long as the app has been allowed to rotate. It only ever happened in that
one direction, which is what gave the cause away: the drawer is positioned in
pixels, and a wider screen moves the closed position out from under it, so it
settled to whichever end it was then nearer.
