Two things this build does that the last one would not.

A NEWSPAPER PAGE THAT PRINTS LAST WEEK'S ANSWER NOW READS THE PUZZLE

Photograph a syndicated newspaper sudoku and you usually photograph two grids:
this week's puzzle, and last week's solution printed underneath it at about half
the size. The app read the solution.

It had a reason. Faced with more than one grid it kept the best one, and the
solution grid is the better grid by every measure it had: it is filled with clean
print and nothing crosses its rules, where the puzzle you are solving has an
answer written in every square and pencilled candidates in the corners. Measured
on such a page the solution scored 0.875 and the puzzle 0.681, so the app
straightened the wrong one and read out last week's answers.

It now keeps the biggest grid rather than the best one, among those that are
clearly grids at all. Size is the one thing that says what you meant: you pointed
the camera at the puzzle you are solving, so the one you meant is the one filling
the frame. Nothing merely large can win this way - the app asks a grid for the
twenty rules a grid must have, and the border of a page or the edge of a table has
none of them and scores zero.

MORE GRIDS ARE FOUND WHEN ONE RULE IS MISSING

The app already forgave a single missing rule, on the sensible ground that a rule
nobody can see is still where the grid says it is - a thumb over a corner, a
highlight across a screen. But it only forgave it when the grid had been found by
tracing its outline, and not when the grid had been found by its cells instead,
which is what happens whenever the border is too faint or too broken to trace.

That was the wrong way round if anything: a grid found by its cells is fitted to
scores of them and knows where its edges are rather better than a traced outline
does. Both now get the same allowance. A puzzle with a hairline border, whose
bottom rule fell a shade outside what the app had fitted, went from being refused
outright to being read.
