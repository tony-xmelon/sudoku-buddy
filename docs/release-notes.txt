A SUDOKU APP ON A SCREEN IS TWO POPULATIONS, NOT ONE

Photograph a sudoku app part way through and every digit on the page is printed -
the clues and your own entries alike, in one face at one size. The app read all
thirty digits on such a page correctly and then filed all thirty as clues, so it
came back with "the printed digits do not make a solvable puzzle" and blamed its
own reading for it.

The clues on that page are drawn at full height and the entries at about
five-sixths of it, which the reader had in fact spotted and sorted correctly. What
undid it was a rule meant for something else. Some fonts set their figures short -
a 1 and a 2 at the height of a lower-case letter - so when the app finds a group
of short digits that are all the same height and as dark as the print, it takes
them back as print. Seven entries in one weight look exactly like that.

The page now gets the last word. A sudoku's clues have exactly one solution, so a
group taken back as print that leaves the clues with no solution was not print. It
is only taken back when the page can still be a puzzle afterwards.

Worth saying plainly about the page that found this: it has a mistake on it. One
entry is a 2 where the answer is a 9, made before the photograph was taken. So the
thirty digits really do not make a puzzle, and an app that reads every one of them
correctly is still right to say so - but it must say the answer is wrong, not that
its own reading is.

THE AMBER OUTLINE IS GONE FROM THE VIEWFINDER

The camera drew two shapes: green for a grid it would accept, amber for the
closest thing it could otherwise find. The amber one was drawn on nearly every
frame, jumped about as the camera moved, and covered the page you were lining up.
It is gone. The green outline appears exactly when there is something to know,
which is what makes it worth seeing.
